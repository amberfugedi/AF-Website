# Who comes through the site, and what they hit

2026-11-20. Written because Amber asked: "I don't want a small business
to get overwhelmed." It is an audit of the site as it stands today, not
a proposal to repositions anything.

The short version: the site does serve the small business, and the
homepage opens by speaking directly to them. What has shifted is
everything a visitor meets *after* that first screen. Two briefs in a
row, employer positioning and then executive recruitment, each correct
on its own terms, have moved the proof and the portfolio toward larger
organizations. Nothing was removed. The centre of gravity moved.

---

## The four people

### P1. The small or local service business

Who they actually are, from the client roster: A2 Exteriors, Robert
James Restoration, Amethyst Aesthetics, Active Chiropractor, Andrea's
Bridal, Nutmeg Technologies. Usually the owner. Often no marketing
person at all.

They arrive saying what Anthony Aubrey says in his testimonial, which is
on the homepage: **"I knew I needed to do more with marketing, but I was
honestly overwhelmed and didn't know where to start."**

What they need to see: that this is normal, that it can be made simple,
and that someone has done it for a business their size.

### P2. The business leader with a marketing function

MembersFirst, StormwaterONE, Scan-Optics in shape. There are marketers.
Something is not working, or nobody is leading it. They are buying
fractional leadership or consulting.

### P3. The recruiter or hiring team

Evaluating Amber for a senior marketing leadership role. Named directly
in the last two briefs. They want scope, scale, outcomes and judgment,
and they read the case studies end to end.

### P4. The marketing leader, peer to peer

Mentorship and advisory. The Secret Marketing Society. Wants a thinking
partner, not a vendor.

## The three places P1 meets work that is not theirs

**1. The homepage proof chips.** All three are mid-market or enterprise
figures: 421% growth in marketing-sourced bookings, pipeline from $1.3M
to $2.8M, 95% more qualified leads. A pressure-washing company reads
"$2.8M pipeline" and concludes this practice is not for them, before
reaching the testimonial that is literally about a pressure-washing
company.

There is no approved small-business figure to put beside them. Inventing
one was proposed and declined on 2026-11-18, correctly. So the honest
options are to leave the chips as they are, or to let one of the three
carry something other than a number.

**2. The homepage Selected Work grid.** Six cards: PERQ, business
intelligence, Scan-Optics, MembersFirst, marketing operations, The Log.
Five of the six are in-house work at companies with marketing
departments. The one client is a credit union. Swapping a single card
for A2 Exteriors or Robert James takes the ratio from 5:1 to 4:2 and
costs nothing but the choice.

**3. The Work page order.** The categories run leadership, client,
systems, independent. "All work" is the default, which is right, but a
visitor who does not already know Amber's own taxonomy has to work out
that "Consulting and client work" is the one that means them.

## The gap in "What sounds like you?"

Services already has the right device: four situations, each routing to
the service that fits. They are:

1. My team is busy and I'm still in every decision
2. We have one problem we can't get past
3. The way the work happens is creating more work
4. You might be the only marketer, new to leading one, or running the
   business and making marketing calls with no one to check them against

**None of them is "I don't know where to start."** Situation 04 comes
closest and still assumes marketing is already happening. That exact
phrase is in Amber's own A2 testimonial and is the stated audience for
Build Your Marketing Foundation. It is the one situation the site can
describe in the client's own words and currently does not.

Adding a fifth situation, or rewording 04, closes it. That is a copy
decision and it is Amber's.

## What to do about the Work page specifically

This was the question that started it, and the persona lens settles it.

**No dropdowns.** Collapsing the four categories means a visitor meets
four labels and no work. That only helps someone who already knows which
door they want, which is precisely what P1 does not know. It also
reintroduces the open-and-close interaction that was deliberately taken
off this page.

**Do not default to a single category.** The earlier recommendation here
was to land on Marketing leadership and growth, because it is the
strongest work and the shortest view. Under the persona lens that is the
worst possible landing for P1: a 421% figure and an organizational design
case study, with no sign that the practice ever works with a business
their size. Withdrawn.

**Keep All work as the default, and add a situation router.** One line
under the page head, three short links, each setting a tab: running a
small business, leading a marketing team, hiring. About 70px, and it
answers "which of these is me" for every persona without hiding
anything.

**Tighten the section rhythm.** 702px of the 6,826px page is section
padding, and three of the four joints between categories are 208px. The
design system already allows a tighter pairing where sections form one
argument. About 240px on desktop, 150px on a phone, no content change.
