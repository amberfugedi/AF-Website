Black streaks on your roof: what causes them and how to clean them

By A2 Exteriors · {{PUBLISH_DATE}}

Short answer

Black streaks on asphalt shingle roofs are algae, most often a species called Gloeocapsa magma. It feeds on the limestone filler in shingles and grows where the roof stays shaded and damp. You can remove it with a low-pressure wash and a cleaning solution. Pressure washing a shingle roof causes damage.

Why algae grows on roofs

Algae spores travel on the wind and land on every roof. They take hold where moisture lingers: north-facing slopes, sections under trees, and spots that stay wet after rain. Most asphalt shingles contain crushed limestone as filler, and the algae feeds on it. As it grows, it develops a dark outer layer that protects it from the sun. That dark layer is the streak you see from the street.

Streaks usually start near the top of a slope and run down in the direction water drains, because rain spreads the algae as it flows.

Algae, moss, or lichen?

  • Algae shows up as black or dark gray streaks. It’s mostly cosmetic.
  • Moss is green and fuzzy and grows in clumps, often along shingle edges. It holds water against the roof and can lift shingles as it grows.
  • Lichen looks like flat, crusty spots, often pale green or gray. It grips the surface hard and can take granules with it if scraped off.

Does it damage the roof?

Algae alone does little structural harm, but it makes a roof look older and can make the attic warmer, because dark streaks absorb more heat. Moss and lichen cause more trouble because they trap moisture. Over time that moisture breaks down shingles and can work its way under them.

How roofs get cleaned

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association advises against pressure washing asphalt shingle roofs. High pressure strips the protective granules. The safer method is a soft wash: a cleaning solution applied at low pressure that kills the algae and moss, followed by a gentle rinse. Algae streaks usually lighten the same day. Moss dies on contact but can take a few weeks of rain to let go.

Keeping it from coming back

  • Trim branches that shade the roof, so it dries faster after rain.
  • Keep gutters clear so water drains off the roof edge.
  • Install zinc or copper strips near the ridge. Rain carries traces of the metal down the roof and slows regrowth.
  • When you replace the roof, ask about algae-resistant shingles, which contain copper granules.
