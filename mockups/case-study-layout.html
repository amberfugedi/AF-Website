The template is built for a long read and half the cases are short reads. The prose column also uses only 63% of its container, so the right side of every page is empty from the band to the footer. No amount of spacing fixes either of those.

And there is a copy problem underneath the layout problem. On Scan-Optics the intro and “The opportunity” say the same thing in different words, so roughly half of those 64 words are a restatement. Both directions below fix the layout. The duplication needs a copy pass whichever one you pick.

A — One spread, with a rail that holds

The head, the facts and the figures stop being three stacked full-width bands and become one spread: prose on the left, a rail on the right carrying role, industry, focus and the outcomes. On a long case the rail sticks while the prose scrolls. The empty right half disappears because there is no longer a right half.

Short cases read as dense rather than stretched, and the same template still carries a 600-word case without changing shape.

B — One composed panel, for the short cases only

A different answer to the same problem: stop pretending a short case is a long page. The whole case becomes one bordered panel about a screen tall, and the page is honestly short rather than stretched. The long cases keep the current full-page template.

The cost is two templates to maintain, and a reader moving between Scan-Optics and PERQ meets two different objects.

Either way

Layout is half of it. Four of the ten pages have no image, no quote and no closing beat — just a situation, a list of what was built, and three numbers. Scan-Optics and StormwaterONE each need one more real thing: what the business could do afterwards, a line from someone who was there, or a piece of the work. That is content only you have, and no template hides its absence.
