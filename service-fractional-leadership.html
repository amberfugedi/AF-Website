Fractional leadership

Stronger marketing doesn’t always start with more marketing.

Sometimes the team is already there. So are the tools, agencies, campaigns, and budget.

But priorities keep shifting. The team stays busy. Leadership isn’t sure what’s actually working. Planning is reactive. Ownership gets blurry. And adding another tactic probably isn’t going to fix it.

I step in as a senior marketing leader to help strengthen the function itself: the direction, people, systems, decisions, and operating rhythm behind the work.

Talk through what’s happening See how I work

You may not need another marketing hire.

You may need a clearer way for the marketing you already have to work.

  • A capable team that spends most of its time reacting.
  • Too many priorities and no clear way to decide what comes first.
  • Marketing activity without enough visibility into what it is producing.
  • A leader who needs stronger systems, structure, or support.
  • Work spread across employees, agencies, freelancers, and tools without clear ownership.
  • Budget being spent because it always has been rather than because it is still working.
  • Reporting that shows activity but does not help leadership make decisions.
  • Sales and marketing working toward slightly different definitions of success.
  • AI being used inconsistently, or added without changing the process around it.
  • Good people operating inside a system that makes their jobs harder than they need to be.

These aren’t always campaign problems. They’re often marketing-function problems.

What fractional leadership looks like

This isn’t a strategy deck handed over from the outside.

I work closely enough with the business and team to understand how the work actually happens, where it gets stuck, and what needs to change.

  • Direction and priorities

    Clarifying what marketing is responsible for, what matters most now, and how the team’s work connects to business goals.

  • Team and accountability

    Creating clearer ownership, expectations, decision-making, and operating rhythms so the team can move more proactively.

  • Budget and resources

    Looking at what you’re already spending across people, agencies, channels, and tools before automatically recommending more.

  • Planning and execution

    Building practical plans the team can actually run, then staying close enough to the work to adjust when reality changes.

  • Marketing operations and reporting

    Improving systems, workflows, CRM processes, measurement, and reporting so the team and leadership have better information to work from.

  • Sales and marketing alignment

    Strengthening handoffs, shared definitions, enablement, communication, and the systems connecting demand to revenue.

  • Workflow and AI

    Finding work that can be simplified, removed, or improved first, then using AI and automation where they genuinely make the team more effective.

  • Team development

    Helping marketers strengthen their judgment, use new tools well, communicate their work, and become less dependent on outside support over time.

Start with what you already have.

More isn’t automatically better.

Before recommending another hire, platform, agency, campaign, or increase in spend, I want to understand what the business is already paying for and whether it’s being used well.

Sometimes the answer is adding something. Sometimes it’s stopping something. Sometimes it’s changing who owns it, how it gets measured, or the process around it.

The goal is not to make the marketing function bigger.

It’s to make it work better.

How I work

  1. 01

    Understand the function

    I start with the business goals, team, current work, budget, tools, reporting, agency relationships, sales process, and the way decisions actually get made. Not just what the org chart or marketing plan says is happening.

  2. 02

    Find the real constraints

    We identify what’s creating the biggest drag on performance. That might be strategy, positioning, leadership, priorities, a workflow, reporting, team structure, sales alignment, or a combination of several things.

  3. 03

    Build what needs to change

    Then we put the right structure around it. That could mean a new operating rhythm, clearer responsibilities, a different planning process, better reporting, revised strategy, improved workflows, team coaching, or hands-on marketing execution.

  4. 04

    Make it usable without me

    I document, teach, coach, and build alongside the team so the improvements become part of how the business operates. The goal isn’t dependence on a fractional leader forever. It’s a stronger marketing function.

What should be different

The exact work varies by company, but the direction is consistent.

Before
  • Marketing feels reactive.
  • Leadership struggles to see what’s producing value.
  • The team is busy but stretched across too many priorities.
  • Decisions depend heavily on one person.
  • Tools and processes have accumulated over time.
  • Budget conversations start with how much more is needed.
After
  • Priorities are clearer.
  • Ownership is easier to understand.
  • The team knows what it is working toward and why.
  • Leadership has better visibility into performance.
  • Planning and reporting support decisions instead of creating more work.
  • The business gets more from the people, budget, systems, and tools already in place.
  • The marketing team is better equipped to keep improving it.

This is probably a fit if…

  • You already have marketers but need stronger leadership around the function.
  • Marketing feels more reactive than strategic.
  • You’re questioning whether the current structure is working.
  • You need senior marketing leadership but aren’t ready for, or don’t need, another full-time executive.
  • You’re spending meaningful money on marketing but aren’t confident you’re getting enough from it.
  • The team needs better systems, priorities, accountability, or executive alignment.
  • You’re going through growth, leadership change, restructuring, or a shift in strategy.
  • You want an experienced outside perspective that can challenge the way things are currently done.
It probably isn’t a fit if…

You’re primarily looking for someone to execute a predetermined list of marketing tasks without questioning the strategy behind them. The value of this work is in looking across the function, challenging assumptions, and making changes where they’re actually needed.

The engagement

Fractional leadership is a retained engagement built around the needs of the business.

Some companies need an ongoing senior marketing leader. Others need someone to step in for a period of time, strengthen the function, coach the team, build the right systems, and help determine what leadership should look like next.

I keep the number of retained engagements small so I can stay close to the work.
