00How to use this book

One system, many pages.

Everything in here was taken from the live site rather than designed for the book. The swatches are the tokens, the buttons are the buttons, the icons are the marks the pages ship. If something looks different on the site than it does here, the site is right and this book is out of date.

The two principles

Consistency of system, not sameness of page. Pages are allowed to look different from each other. The same content role is not allowed to be styled differently for no reason. A card title is a card title everywhere.

Every visual choice should improve understanding, hierarchy or personality. If it does none of those three, remove it. This is the test that settles most arguments, and it is the reason the site has as much empty space as it does.

Who this is for

A designer, developer, contractor or agency who needs to add something to the brand without it reading as a bolt-on. Hand them this book and they can match the system without reading a stylesheet.

It is also the reference for anything off-site: a deck, a one pager, a worksheet, a social template, a printed piece. The rules here travel.

Where the system actually lives

FileRoleWhat it holds
styles.cssThe build The single stylesheet all sixteen pages share. The tokens are in :root at the top.
design-system/DIGITAL‑SYSTEM.mdNormative What is true now: tokens, component rules, and the judgement calls about when to reach for something. Read this first.

There is no design file. The site is sixteen hand-written HTML pages with no framework and no build step, and the stylesheet is the source of truth. That is deliberate: one place to change a value, and no drift between a mockup and what ships.

01The brand

A marketing leader who also builds.

The brand has to hold two things at once: senior enough to be trusted with a function, close enough to the work to be trusted with the build. Every design decision below is downstream of that.

What the site says she does

Marketing leader, strategist and builder. More than a decade turning complicated business problems into marketing that works better. Leads growth marketing in-house and takes on a small number of clients alongside it.

Four ways to work together, and the difference between them is mostly how much of the work she takes on: fractional leadership, consulting, workflow and AI, mentorship and advisory.

What that means visually

  • Warm, not corporate. Cream ground, soft pastel atmosphere, serif voice. It does not look like a SaaS vendor.
  • Editorial, not decorative. Real whitespace, hairlines instead of boxes, one accent colour that means one thing.
  • Personal, not lifestyle. Her own photographs in organic frames. No stock, no props, no desk flatlays.
  • Precise, not playful. Geometric line marks, one stroke weight, no doodles.

The three pillars

Used as the site's own shorthand, including on the share card.

Leading

Stepping into a business and running the marketing function. Direction, priorities, ownership, team.

Consulting

One defined problem, worked out properly, then the build that follows.

Building

The systems behind the work. Workflow, practical AI tools, and training so it still gets used later.

Who reads this site

A business owner or an executive deciding whether they need a marketing leader, a specific problem solved, or a better way of working. They are senior, short on time, and sceptical of marketing language.

Which is why the copy is short, first-person and concrete, and why the design uses whitespace instead of persuasion.

Clear space

Keep the height of the mark clear on all four sides. Nothing crosses it: no rule, no photo edge, no pastel cloud core.

Minimum size

28px for the mark on screen, 0.3in in print. Below that the af closes up. Use the mark alone rather than shrinking the lockup until the name is unreadable.

Do not

  • Set the name in Manrope, or in any face but Newsreader 500.
  • Put the wordmark in coral. Coral is interaction, never identity.
  • Add a tagline to the lockup. The pillars are copy, not part of the mark.
  • Place the lockup on a dark ground. There is no reversed version.
  • Square the mark's corners, or round them to a circle.
  • Stretch, outline, emboss, or add a shadow to either part.
  • Recolour the tile. Its pastel gradient is fixed.

Favicon and app icon

The mark alone, already exported at 16, 32, 48 and 512, plus apple-touch-icon.png. These are the only sanctioned uses of the mark without the name.

Alpha

Alpha belongs in rgba() of a palette RGB, or in fill-opacity on an SVG. rgba(46,42,39,0.06) is the ink token at six percent and is fine. #F7F1EB is a new colour and is not.

04The aura

Four clouds, and they belong to the whole site.

The signature atmosphere: four blurred pastel clouds on a fixed layer behind every page, drifting slowly and sharpening as the page unfolds. It is the single most recognisable thing about the brand and the easiest thing to get wrong.

Each is a radial gradient running to fully transparent at 60–62 percent, with filter: blur(46px) and border-radius: 50%. Green is deliberately the quietest of the four at 0.30 alpha: it is a warm note in the atmosphere, not a fourth equal colour.

Do

  • Let them bleed off the edges. A fully contained cloud reads as a shape, not as air.
  • Overlap them by roughly a third where two meet.
  • Keep green faintest.
  • Check text contrast over the dense part. The core sits at 40–55 percent of the blob, not at its centre.
  • Put them behind everything. They are a background layer, never content.

Do not

  • Add a fifth cloud. There are four.
  • Recolour them, or raise green to match the others.
  • Dim the aura on one page. The layer is site-wide and shared, so changing it changes every page.
  • Give a cloud a hard edge, an outline or a visible stop.
  • Use the aura as a section background where cream would do. It marks heroes, major editorial moments and some closing CTAs.

In print, soft gradients band on some devices. Render the clouds as high-resolution transparent PNGs rather than vectors, and keep the 8:9 silhouettes the site uses.

The hierarchy ladder

On any page, in this order, with no rung skipped upward.

h1  one per page

The local stack

A card title inside a four-column row sits at --fs-body, not --fs-h3. It is still the top of its own local stack, because everything under it is --fs-small.

This is the rule that keeps a dense row from shouting. Four 30px titles in a row outrank the section heading above them, which inverts the page.

05Typography · case and punctuation

Sentence case, almost everywhere.

Capitalise the first word and nothing else. This applies to every heading, label, card title, button and nav item, and it is the rule broken most often by anyone new to the brand.

Title Case is reserved

Only for things that genuinely carry it:

  • Proper nouns: PERQ, Scan-Optics, MembersFirst CT Federal Credit Union
  • Job titles: VP of Growth Marketing & Enablement
  • Named deliverables inside the course: Foundation Audit, Foundation Plan
  • Page names in the nav: My Approach

Everything else is sentence case, including a heading's second sentence, which of course capitalises its own first word.

Tags, eyebrows and footer heads are set uppercase with text-transform, so their source case never shows and does not matter.

Punctuation in headings

A heading that is a sentence gets a period. A heading that is a label does not. Both are correct and the site uses both deliberately: 27 percent of its 243 headings end in a period.

09Cards and panels

Five containers. Each has a job.

Reaching for the wrong one is the most common structural mistake in this system. The question is never "does this look nice in a card", it is "does this content need containing at all".

White card

White, 1px line, 24px radius. An offer, a case, a thing you can click. The only container that takes a lift on hover.

Warm card

White at 52 percent, 1px line, 16px radius. Scope items in a two-by-two. Quieter than a white card because it is not a destination.

Soft explainer panel

Page tint at six percent or less, 1px line, 24px radius, no shadow. A contained comparison at aside scale.

Fit panel

Service tint over white, 1px line, 24px radius. The one "is this you" moment per page.

Border-only container

Transparent, 1px line, 24px radius. Grouping without weight, for when the content belongs together but nothing should look raised.

Not a card

  • Narrative copy.
  • How it works stages.
  • A list of four short facts.
  • A CTA.
  • Testimonials, unless there is a strong reason.
  • About-page personal storytelling.

The test

A card is a promise that the thing inside it is a discrete object you could act on. Four short facts are not four objects, they are one idea in four parts, and they read better as editorial columns with a hairline and a small mark.

This exact change was made on the About page: a four-item card grid became four columns with a line icon, a thin rule and a serif fact, and the section got quieter and more confident.

11Photography

Her own photographs, in organic frames.

The frame carries the meaning. An organic radius says this is Amber. A plain rectangle says this is work. Those two treatments are never swapped.

Organic frameAmber's own photographs. 8:9, 55% 45% 47% 53% / 47% 52% 48% 53%, 1px line, soft shadow, and an offset 2px coral outline rotated −3 degrees.

Landscape variant5:4 for landscape sources, so the frame does not crop the scene away. Opposite pairs still sum to 100 percent.

Rectangle, 8pxWork artifacts and screenshots. 16:10, top-cropped. A client build is someone else's brand and stays on the Projects page.

Rules

  • No photograph is used twice across the site. Each page gets its own.
  • No stock photography, ever. If there is no real photograph, use whitespace.
  • No props that are not genuinely hers: no coffee mugs staged for the shot, no plants, no notebook flatlays.
  • Crop to keep the working scene. A centred crop that hides the desk, the laptop and the notebook loses the reason the photo was chosen.
  • Two photographs of her at similar scale side by side read as one picture doubled. Vary the scale hard, or use one.

Photos, graphics or whitespace

PhotosTrust, personality, lifestyle, human connection.
GraphicsExplanation, structure, transformation, systems.
WhitespaceWhen neither adds value.

Do not fill whitespace automatically. A visual earns its place. This is the same test as the guiding principle: if it does not improve understanding, hierarchy or personality, it comes out.

Every image carries alt text and explicit width and height. Decorative images take alt=""; decorative inline SVG takes aria-hidden.

12Explainer graphics

Use one when a visual beats more copy.

That is the whole purpose: an explainer graphic is used when a picture makes a business idea easier to understand than another paragraph would. Not when the page has a gap.

Use one when

  • It can replace explanatory paragraphs.
  • The idea is a clear relationship or transformation.
  • It helps distinguish one service from another.
  • Desktop whitespace can be used meaningfully.
  • The concept is easier to scan than to read.

Good subjects: transformation, before and after, current state to better state, problem to solution, friction, simplification, relationship, process.

Do not when

  • The copy is already clear.
  • It is only filling space.
  • A photograph would communicate trust better.
  • It duplicates the How it works content nearby.
  • It adds another card or grid pattern the page does not need.
  • It makes the page feel like SaaS product marketing.

Two devices, one family

The soft explainer panel is for a comparison that sits beside copy as an aside: contained, at aside scale, 1px line, 24px radius, page tint, no shadow. A 22px icon column, compact rows, a thin arrow between panels at 0.75 opacity.

The editorial comparison is the same job at section scale, and deliberately not the same device: two columns of hairline rows, no box, no fill, a dash marking each "before" and a ring in the page accent marking each "after".

Boxing a full-width comparison turns two columns into two large cards and loses the page's air. Un-boxing an aside loses the containment that made it readable next to prose.

Someone should recognise them as related without meeting the identical component on every page. That is the rule for the whole system, stated once.

Characteristics

Editorial, light, warm, simple, practical, restrained. Thin line icons, pale tinted panels, fine rules, subtle arrows, limited text inside the graphic, generous whitespace.

Arrows

Only for real movement, flow, sequence or relationship. Thin, subtle, secondary. Never decoration.

Handwritten accents

Rare. At most one small annotation per major page, and zero is fine. Only to land a takeaway or add a personal note. Never for headings, body copy, instructions, navigation, or two adjacent components. It must never become a scrapbook theme.

Desktop split

Copy left, visual right, at roughly 1.7fr to 1fr. On mobile, stack only if the graphic is still useful; otherwise hide it. Never shrink a detailed graphic until it is unreadable.

13Page families and responsive

Same families, different content.

Every service page runs the same section families in the same order. What varies is the accent, the graphics, the imagery and the examples, never the structure.

The service page order

hero  →  scope  →  fit  →  How it works  →  proof  →  CTA

  • How it works. The heading is exactly "How it works" on all four pages. Serif heading, concise intro, three to four stages on fine rules, small line icons, serif stage headings, sans copy. No process circles, no chunky cards, no heavy arrows, and never rigid sequence language for an ongoing engagement.
  • Scope. The unnumbered two-by-two warm-card grid. It says what the engagement can touch. Not numbered, because numbers mean sequence and sequence is How it works' job.
  • Fit. One panel per page. Specific scenarios, neutral open states, contextual links.
  • Philosophy does not live here. It lives on My Approach.

About and My Approach

They share heading sizes, reading widths, section spacing, icon style, graphic line weight and background system. They differ on purpose: About is personal, visual and human. My Approach is editorial, thought-led and principle-driven.

14Motion

Two devices, and the budget is closed.

Motion on this site does one thing: it lets an idea arrive in the order it should be read. It never announces itself, and there is no third effect coming.

The reveal

Content fades and rises into place as it enters the viewport, with an optional stagger so a row arrives left to right rather than all at once. Easing is cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1), the single ease token --ease.

The draw-in

Line marks draw themselves. Every stroke carries pathLength="100" so one dasharray rule drives every mark in the system: the stroke draws over 1.2s, then any pastel fill fades in behind it at 0.55s delay.

Alpha on a fill must live in fill-opacity or rgba(), never opacity. The fill animation ends at opacity: 1 with forwards and overrides it.

Rules

  • All motion respects prefers-reduced-motion. Under it the aura is static and content is simply present.
  • Reuse the two devices rather than adding a third. The motion budget is closed.
  • Nothing moves on a loop in the reader's eyeline. The aura drifts over 14 to 21 seconds, which is slow enough to read as atmosphere.
  • Hover is 0.16 to 0.25s. Anything slower feels broken.
  • Measure motion over time, not in a single frame. A screenshot taken 500ms into a 2.2s cascade looks like a bug and is not one.

16Accessibility

The floor is 4.5:1, and it is not negotiable.

Most of the palette's shape comes from this constraint. Coral exists twice, once as a fill and once as text, because the fill version cannot carry type on cream.

What is enforced

  • No text below 4.5:1 against whatever is actually behind it, including the dense core of an aura cloud.
  • Coral and the pastels are fill only. Text on a pastel takes that pastel's -deep partner.
  • One h1 per page, and no heading level skipped.
  • Every image has alt text and explicit dimensions. Decorative images take an empty alt; decorative inline SVG takes aria-hidden.
  • Focus is always visible: a 2px --coral-deep outline at 3px offset.
  • A skip link, hidden until focused.
  • Touch targets 44px minimum.
  • Colour never carries meaning alone. Links are coloured and underlined.
  • All motion respects prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Forms are keyboard-friendly, with real labels and consent stated at the point of collection.

How to check it

Do not compute contrast against the token you think is behind the text. Compute it against the rendered pixels. The aura sits behind most of the site, its dense core is off-centre, and a value that passes on cream can fail on a cloud.

This is not hypothetical. An eyebrow measured 4.37:1 on a launch graphic because a pink cloud's core landed under it. The token was correct; the composition was not.

Legal and compliance

The site carries a privacy policy, terms, and a cookie policy. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, loads no trackers and embeds no third-party content, so no consent banner is required. If any of those four things changes, the banner becomes mandatory.

Form consent is stated where data is collected. Any statistic used as a claim has to be substantiable, and no review or testimonial is used that was not given.

17Never do this

The list that keeps the system honest.

Each of these was either tried and reverted, or shipped and fixed. They are here because they are the things that look fine in a mockup and wrong on the site.

Type

  • A third font family, for any reason.
  • Weight 700 or 800.
  • Title Case on a heading, label, button or nav item.
  • A size outside the scale and its five exceptions.
  • Letter-spacing in pixels.

Colour

  • Coral as text on cream. Use --coral-deep.
  • Coral as a divider, decoration or category code.
  • A third colour-coding set.
  • A text grey that is not ink, body, muted or a -deep accent.
  • A new hex where an existing token at an alpha would do.

Surface

  • White cards on a white section.
  • A border and a shadow on the same card.
  • Glassmorphism, coloured glow, heavy drop shadows.
  • A radius outside the six.

Imagery

  • Stock photography.
  • The same photograph on two pages.
  • Staged props: mugs, plants, notebook flatlays.
  • An organic frame on a work artifact, or a rectangle on one of her own photographs.
  • A client's build used anywhere but Projects.

Marks and graphics

  • Doodles. A mug, a vase, a lightbulb, a puzzle piece.
  • Tape, pins, sticky notes, layered paper, scrapbook textures.
  • Two stroke weights in one mark.
  • Shipping a mark without viewing it at 5×.
  • An arrow that is not showing movement.
  • More than one handwritten accent on a page.

Structure

  • Putting narrative copy, a CTA, or four short facts in cards.
  • Numbering the scope grid.
  • Renaming "How it works" on any service page.
  • Adding a motion device.
  • Shrinking a detailed graphic instead of hiding it on mobile.
