Workflow and AI

Not every repetitive task needs AI. Some of it just needs to stop.

Most marketing teams are carrying work nobody chose. A report that took an hour once and takes four now. A handoff that exists because someone left. A tool three people log into.

I help teams see how the work actually happens, take out what isn’t earning its place, and then automate or apply AI where there’s a real case for it.

Show me what the team is doing See what I’ve built

The work about the work

None of this is dramatic. It just quietly takes a day a week off a team that doesn’t have one to spare.

  • The same information gets typed into three places.
  • Reporting takes hours every week and still doesn’t answer the question.
  • Nobody is certain which document is the current one.
  • Approvals add days without adding judgment.
  • Tools were bought that nobody fully uses.
  • People are using AI on their own, with no shared approach.
  • Work moves between marketing and sales by hand.
  • Automation has been layered on top of a process that was already broken.

Most workflows carry work nobody needs, and nobody has been told they can stop.

Fix the process before automating it.

AI helps where the work repeats. Everywhere else it can just add a step.

So before anything gets automated, we go through the same short list:

  • Should this work exist at all?
  • Does it need to happen this often?
  • Does this information already live somewhere else?
  • Is the bottleneck really the tool, or is it ownership?
  • Will the team actually use this once it’s built?

The test is whether the team still uses it after I leave.

How the work goes

  1. 01

    Watch how the work actually happens

    Not the documented process. The one people are running, including the spreadsheet nobody mentions in meetings.

  2. 02

    Find the friction

    Where things wait, get re-entered, get chased, or get done twice because two people weren’t sure who owned it.

  3. 03

    Simplify first

    Steps come out, ownership gets clearer, and some work stops entirely. This is usually where most of the time comes back.

  4. 04

    Automate what earns it

    Then connect the systems, build the automation, or apply AI to the parts that genuinely repeat.

  5. 05

    Train and document it

    A workflow nobody understands gets abandoned the first time it breaks. The team needs to be able to run it, change it, and fix it without me.

What should be different

Mostly measured in hours the team gets back.

Before
  • Manual reporting every week.
  • AI experiments happening everywhere.
  • Several versions of the same information.
  • More tools.
After
  • A repeatable workflow that takes minutes.
  • A few use cases the team actually trusts.
  • One version people can find.
  • Less work.

This is probably a fit if…

  • Your team spends too much of the week on work that repeats.
  • You think AI could help but don’t want it forced into everything.
  • Your processes have collected steps over the years that nobody has revisited.
  • Handoffs between marketing and sales are still manual.
  • Reporting costs more effort than the decisions it supports.
  • People are experimenting with AI and you want a shared, practical approach.
  • You suspect the answer is simpler than buying another tool.
It probably isn’t a fit if…

You want an AI strategy as a headline rather than a change to how the work happens. The useful version of this is unglamorous, and most of the gain comes from the steps that get removed before anything is built.

The engagement

This can be a workflow audit, a process redesign, identifying where AI genuinely fits, building the automations, or ongoing improvement as the team changes.

Training and documentation are usually part of it rather than an add-on, because adoption is the whole point. If you want the team taught something specific, that can be built into the engagement.
