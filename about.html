I’ve never been very good at staying inside the marketing box.

I’m Amber. I’ve spent more than a decade in marketing, but the work I’m most drawn to usually starts somewhere between marketing, sales, operations, technology, and the question nobody has asked yet.

Marketing leader. Strategist. Builder. Always asking why.

How I think

I usually start with the problem underneath the problem.

  • Give me a website problem and I’ll probably start asking about sales.
  • Tell me you need more leads and I’ll want to know what happens to them after they come in.
  • Show me a process everyone hates and I’m going to wonder why everyone is still doing it.

Either way, I want you to understand why I am recommending it. I’ll tell you what I think you need, and I’ll tell you when I don’t think you need something.

I like figuring out what is actually going on, then making it better.

Sometimes that answer is a campaign. Sometimes it is a new website.

Sometimes it is fixing what happens after someone fills out the form. And sometimes the answer is that we should stop doing something altogether.

What I believe

What I keep coming back to.

01

Facts over feelings.

I like opinions. I just like them more when we can test them.

What are customers actually doing? Where are people getting stuck? What is sales actually hearing? What does the data say compared with what we assumed was happening?

The interesting part usually starts when those answers do not match.

02

Strategy only matters if it gets built.

A recommendation that never meets a real constraint tends to stay on the page.

Once you write the actual words, build the workflow, launch the campaign, or explain the change to the person who has to live with it, you find out which parts of the strategy were actually true.

03

Make the work lighter.

Most businesses carry work nobody really needs, and nobody has been told they can stop doing.

Extra approvals. Duplicated documents. Reports nobody reads. Processes that grew one step at a time until they became ridiculous.

I’m always looking for some version of: can this be simpler? AI can help where the work repeats. Everywhere else, it can just as easily add another step.

I’m a strategy person who still wants to touch the work.

I like the big-picture part of marketing. Figuring out where the business is going, what needs to change, how the pieces connect, and what is worth prioritizing.

But I’ve never wanted to become the person who only hands someone else the deck.

  • I still want to look at the landing page.
  • I still want to understand what is happening in the CRM.
  • I still want to know why a workflow has twelve steps when it could have four.

And if we decide something needs to change, I like being close enough to the team to make sure it actually does.

How I lead

The people part matters to me, too.

One of the things I’ve made time for throughout my career is mentoring.

I got meaningful experience early in my own career, and it put me ahead in ways I didn’t fully appreciate at the time. Someone trusted me with real work before I had years of experience proving I could do it.

I’ve tried to pass that on.

I want people in the conversation. I want them to understand why decisions are being made, ask questions, try things themselves, get feedback, and leave knowing more than they did when they started.

I think investing in people is one of the best uses of my time.

I want people to leave more capable than when we started.

What keeps me interested

What keeps me interested isn’t really marketing.

It’s the moment when something complicated finally makes sense.

When the numbers explain what everyone has been feeling.

When the team suddenly knows what to do next.

When something that wasn’t working starts working.

When a process gets easier because someone finally asked why it existed in the first place.

Or when everyone realizes we can stop doing the thing nobody wanted to do anyway.

That’s the part I like. The place where curiosity, people, ideas, and real problems turn into something useful.

Outside the work

And outside the marketing of it all…

I’ve always been curious about why things are the way they are.

Why something works. Why it doesn’t. Why people do things a certain way. Why I reacted the way I did. Most of my questions are really me trying to understand the why.

That curiosity tends to spill into everything else. I like learning, making things, and following ideas far enough to see what they could become. I’ve started far too many side hustles over the years. Some became real things. Some became websites. Some lived briefly in a Notes app. The upside is that I have absolutely no shortage of ideas.

I’m very self-aware. Sometimes probably more than is useful.

I care a lot about growth, both my own and other people’s. I like learning, changing my mind when I need to, and giving people the time and opportunity to get better at what they do.

I also get invested. In the work, in people, and in the things I choose to put my energy into.

I’ve learned that ambition and taking care of myself can’t be separate things. Fitness, movement, yoga, and my mental health are important parts of my life. I’m ambitious, but I don’t think burnout is a personality trait.

I’m based in Connecticut, have two dogs, and have fostered dogs over the years.

I want to leave things better than I found them.

A team. A business. Someone’s career. Myself.

I’m not always going to get that right, but it’s the direction I want to keep moving in.
