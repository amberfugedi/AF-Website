Leading marketing

I enjoy building teams, brands, and marketing functions that can grow with the business.

B2B SaaS · PropTech · Multifamily

PERQ

Growing a marketing function alongside the business, and building the team and capabilities to match where it was heading.

Promoted twice in just over two years, with a widening remit across the business.

Read case studyClose case study↓

How it progressed

I joined in December 2023 as Director of Demand Generation, moved to Director of Growth Marketing and Enablement, and was promoted to VP in January 2026. At the end of 2024 the whole marketing function came under my ownership, and I shaped the department around where the business was going next.

What I own

The function end to end, from brand and positioning through demand generation to marketing operations, with sales enablement inside a wider remit. I create the roles the business needs and develop the people in them, direct where the investment goes, and build the internal tooling that makes performance easier to read. I work with the executive team on corporate strategy and annual planning, and the results below came from the whole team and the wider business working toward the same targets.

The call

I decided what the function needed to be before deciding who should be in it: which work belonged inside the team and which outside it, which roles were worth creating, and where the investment would do the most good.

Impact

  • 421%growth in marketing-sourced new customer bookings2026 to date, through October, against the same period in 2025
  • 2.6xmedian marketing-sourced new customer booking value2025 to 2026
  • 119%more organic MQLs2025
  • 30%less of the budget going to outside professional services2026 forecast, year over year

What it changed about how I lead. The hardest part was not the marketing. It was learning to start from what the business needed next, design the team around that, and then make the investment calls that followed. I also learned that being the most capable person in the room is a different thing from being the most useful one in it.

eLearning · Marketing Director

StormwaterONE

Lead generation, nurture automation, and webinars.

More qualified leads on a smaller paid budget.

Read case studyClose case study↓

Challenge

A niche eLearning company with an opportunity to bring brand and demand generation into one direction, and to get more out of its paid media investment.

Approach

Nurture automation and personalization, a webinar program that converted registrants into paying students, and a MarTech migration run without breaking business continuity.

The call

More paid spend was the obvious lever, and it was already outrunning its return. I moved the weight to owned demand instead, building webinars and nurture up until they carried the pipeline, which is what let the paid budget come down while qualified leads went up.

Impact

  • 95%more qualified leads2019–2021
  • ~36%less paid media spend2019–2021
  • 35%higher impression share2019–2021

Business systems and intelligence

Built inside the businesses I have worked for, and owned by them.

  • Business intelligence and application development Building applications that make complex business information easier to understand and act on. Read case studyClose case study↓

    * An illustrative mock-up. The company, the screens and the figures are invented.

    The opportunity

    The business already had years of marketing data and reporting behind it. Bringing it into one place meant a quarter could be read against the same quarter two years back directly, and that history could inform the next decision.

    What I built

    I worked out what each role needed to decide with, then designed and built the application myself with Claude, a feature at a time, on top of the reporting already in place.

    What it enables

    Every function can reach the same figures, and every page carries the date it was pulled, so the age of a number is never a question. Conversations start from the evidence rather than from assembling it.

  • Marketing operations and organizational development Designing how a marketing department works: priorities, ownership, and the link between strategy and execution. Read case studyClose case study↓

    The opportunity

    The department had deep knowledge of its work and a growing remit. Setting that down in one shared operating model meant the team could scale it, hand it on, and bring new people up to speed quickly.

    What I built

    I designed the operating model and we put it in place together with the team’s marketing operations specialist, so the people who would use it every day helped shape it.

    What it enables

    Objectives, the work beneath them and ownership sit in one place, which makes it possible to prioritize, hand work over, or stop it. New people have a clear place to start from.

  • Strategic financial planning Assessing what past investment returned, weighing alternatives, and planning against a revenue target. Read case studyClose case study↓

    The opportunity

    Years of marketing investment and performance history were there to learn from. Bringing that record into the planning process meant the next year’s investment could be built on what the business had already learned.

    What I built

    I build the analysis underneath the number: what past investment returned, what the alternatives would cost, and the recommendation that goes to the leaders who weigh it.

    What it enables

    Investment conversations start from evidence, so the business can weigh a marketing decision the same way it weighs any other commercial one.
