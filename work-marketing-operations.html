Marketing Leadership & Growth

Designing how a marketing department works

Priorities, ownership, and the link between strategy and execution, plus the planning work that decides where the investment goes.

Context
Built inside an employer and owned by them
Role
Designed the operating model, led the planning
Focus
Operating model, ownership and prioritization, budgeting and forecasting, resource allocation, scenario planning, historical performance analysis
Built with
The team’s marketing operations specialist

The opportunity

The department had deep knowledge of its work and a growing remit. Setting that down in one shared operating model meant the team could scale it, hand it on, and bring new people up to speed quickly.

What I built

I designed the operating model and we put it in place together with the team’s marketing operations specialist, so the people who would use it every day helped shape it.

What it enables

Objectives, the work beneath them and ownership sit in one place, which makes it possible to prioritize, hand work over, or stop it. New people have a clear place to start from.

The investment side of the same job

Years of marketing investment and performance history were there to learn from. Bringing that record into the annual planning process meant the next year’s marketing budget could be built on what the business had already learned.

I build the analysis underneath the marketing budget: benchmarking against previous years, modelling how different allocations would perform, running scenarios against the revenue target, and taking a recommendation to the leaders who weigh it.

Investment conversations start from evidence, so the business can weigh a marketing decision the same way it weighs any other commercial one.
