Section purpose

This section should help visitors quickly recognize the kind of support they need.

It must account for:

* an established business with little or no formal marketing
* a founder or leader carrying too much of the marketing
* a business with a team that is active but not aligned
* a marketer, founder, or leader who needs experienced guidance

The options are based on the visitor’s situation, not company size.

Section heading

Keep:

What sounds like you?

Update the supporting copy to:

Start with the situation that feels closest. The right kind of support depends less on the size of the business and more on what the work needs now.

This should feel practical and inclusive, not like a personality quiz.

Selector labels

Use these four labels:

1. We have no real marketing foundation
2. Too much of the marketing depends on me
3. We have a team, but the work is not connecting
4. I need an experienced person in my corner

These are intentionally more specific than the current shortened labels.

On smaller screens, allow the labels to wrap naturally. Do not reduce the font until they become difficult to read.

State 1: We have no real marketing foundation

Main statement

The business works. Marketing has never been clearly built around it.

What may be happening

You may have grown through referrals, reputation, relationships, or the owner’s effort without ever building a clear marketing function. Now you need to understand what matters, what support to hire, and what can wait.

Do not imply that the business is new, unsuccessful, or has no customers.

How I help

I help define the audience, positioning, message, priorities, and practical marketing foundation the business actually needs. Then we decide what can be handled internally, what should be outsourced, and what does not need to be done yet.

Best place to start

Design direction

Keep the current left-selector/right-response structure, but make it feel more connected and intentional.

The section should feel:

* warm
* practical
* approachable
* creative
* clearly interactive
* designed without becoming overly editorial

Use serif only for the main section heading.

Use sans serif for:

* selector labels
* state statement
* descriptions
* section labels
* recommendations
* CTAs

Do not use oversized serif copy inside the selected response.

Active selector state

Use:

* a soft butter background across the active row
* a thin coral rule on the left
* a small coral arrow on the right
* slightly stronger text weight
* a subtle transition

Do not use filled pills, conventional cards, checkmarks, or large icons.

Response area

Use this hierarchy:

1. compact changing artifact
2. main state statement
3. What may be happening
4. How I help
5. Best place to start
6. CTA

The main statement should be clear and conversational, but not styled like a second page hero.

Keep the response integrated into the page. Do not place it inside a large card.

Responsive behavior

On desktop:

* keep the selector on the left
* keep the response on the right
* align both columns at the top
* reduce empty space
* make the changing artifact compact
* keep the content readable without making the section oversized

On mobile:

* stack the selector above the response
* keep all four choices visible
* show the compact artifact above the selected statement
* avoid oversized typography
* keep the CTA visible without excessive scrolling
* avoid horizontal overflow
* keep tap targets accessible

Final checks

Before finishing:

* verify all four states and CTAs
* test keyboard, touch, and focus behavior
* test desktop, tablet, and mobile
* ensure the section does not rely on hover
* ensure there is no layout jump when switching options
* confirm no unrelated page styles changed
* remove obsolete selector markup and styles
* summarize the files modified

The final section should make visitors think, “That is exactly where I am,” and immediately understand how Amber can help.
