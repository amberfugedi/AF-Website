“Where I help”: row hierarchy

What I measured. One screen of this section carries seven text treatments, three of them Newsreader: the 28px section title, the 24px row title, and 19px for a sentence — the contribution copy. A paragraph in the display face, five pixels from the title it is meant to support.

Why type alone cannot fix it. The situation and the contribution sit side by side. Two things at the same altitude read as equal whatever their treatment, so the serif/sans split reads as “different kinds of thing” rather than “one outranks the other”. Ranking by weight works down a column, not across one.

The serif on the contribution is deliberate and documented (“the contribution still reads larger and darker than the situation copy”), so every option below is a way of keeping that intent while making it legible.

Option A — One serif voice

Option B — Stack it, so weight means rank

One column. The contribution sits directly under the title at the intro grade in ink; the situation follows beneath it, smaller and lighter. The documented intent survives intact — the contribution is larger and darker — and now it actually reads that way, because rank runs down the page rather than across it.

Option C — Contribution leads, situation becomes a labelled aside
