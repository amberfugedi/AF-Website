Consulting & client work

Building a marketing engine that supports real business growth.

Community financial institutions need marketing that does more than maintain visibility. It needs to support lending, attract new members and make the most of available resources.

This engagement brings together digital acquisition, content, marketing processes and performance measurement to support a more sustainable approach to growth.

The business challenge

A community credit union carries the same marketing considerations as a much larger lender, with a fraction of the resources to meet them. The work had to hold several of those at once.

  1. Several products, several audiences.A HELOC, a mortgage, an auto loan and a personal loan are bought by different people in different circumstances, and each needs its own reasoning rather than a share of one general campaign.
  2. Members and lending, at the same time.New member acquisition and loan growth pull on the same budget and the same attention, and a plan that serves one well can quietly starve the other.
  3. Consistent content without more overhead.A small team has to produce relevant, on-brand, compliant content every month. The constraint is rarely ideas. It is capacity and review.
  4. A line from digital activity to applications.Impressions and clicks are easy to see. Whether they turn into applications and signed loans is the question the business actually has.
  5. Making the most of what already exists.The useful question is usually not what else to buy. It is what the current budget, channels and team can be made to do.

The approach

Four pillars, each one a way of connecting marketing work to something the credit union was already trying to achieve.

01. Align marketing with business priorities

Marketing planning starts from the lending products the credit union wants to grow and the membership it wants to attract, not from a campaign calendar. That sounds obvious and it changes what gets made: the plan is built around which products need volume, which audiences they sit with, and what the business is trying to do that quarter.

Marketing activity is the means here, not the goal. The goal is the applications and the members behind it.

02. Build a digital acquisition strategy

Digital acquisition has to carry several product categories at once: HELOCs, home loans, auto loans and personal loans. The work covers search and paid campaigns on Google and Meta, the website experiences people land on, and the points where an interested visitor becomes an applicant.

A community lender competes against big-bank budgets, so the advantage has to come from relevance rather than spend: being present for the specific moment a member is thinking about a specific product, and making the next step obvious when they are.

03. Create a content operation that holds

Content for a credit union has to be consistent, on brand, and compliant, every month, from a small team. The monthly content process runs planning, assets, email and compliance checks in one pass, with NCUA review on every asset before it goes anywhere.

The Content Engine supports that process. It helps with development and review so the team spends its time on judgment rather than on the repetitive parts, and it works inside the brand and the review process rather than around them. AI is the enabling resource here, not the strategy. In a financial services environment the human review is the part that cannot be delegated, and the engine is built on that assumption.

No prompts, configurations, personas or internal workflows are published here or anywhere on this site. What sits inside the engine is the client’s.

04. Connect marketing to measurable outcomes

Impressions, clicks and engagement describe how a campaign behaved. They do not tell a lender whether the money is working. The outcomes that matter here are applications, signed loans and new member applications, and the useful question each month is what the reporting says about those.

The credit union maintains the reporting. The contribution is in reading it against the plan: looking at which products are producing applications, where the digital-source volume is going, and what that suggests about where the next month’s effort belongs.

Better marketing isn’t always more marketing.

The instinct, when a lender wants more applications, is to run more campaigns. That works for exactly as long as someone is pushing it.

What lasts is the connection between the parts: a plan tied to the products the business is trying to grow, acquisition that covers each of them, a content process the team can actually sustain, and reporting that says whether any of it is landing. None of those four is impressive on its own. Together they are a marketing function a small team can run without the organization having to keep adding to it.

What I would take into the next one

  • Marketing should connect to business outcomes.Performance is worth evaluating against meaningful organizational objectives, not just channel-level activity.
  • Lean teams need repeatable processes.The right workflows and tools make consistent execution manageable at a size where it otherwise would not be.
  • Technology should support the strategy.AI and other tools are most valuable when they solve a defined business or operational problem.
