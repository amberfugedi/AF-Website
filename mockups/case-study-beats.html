The problem was never the layout

Three attempts rearranged the same content: a situation, a list of what was built, and some numbers. That sequence is a résumé bullet, not a story. Nothing in it was ever at risk, nothing was difficult, and nothing could have gone another way, so there is nothing for a reader to be interested in. No arrangement of boxes fixes that.

A story needs a turn: a moment where it could have gone differently and someone chose. On a portfolio that is the single most valuable thing on the page, because anyone can list HubSpot and ABM. The judgment is the part only you have.

So this is not a container. It is the story, told in beats, with the facts tucked underneath where they belong. Two voices, one column. You can read only the large lines and still get it.

A2 Exteriors — when the work itself is the evidence

Same beats. The artifact takes the evidence slot instead of figures, and the client’s own words do the closing. Nothing about the template changes.

A2 Exteriors · Pressure washing · Consultant

A small pressure washing company was doing good work under a brand that didn’t look like it.

The owner knew marketing mattered and had no idea where to start.

Which is where most small businesses begin, and why most of them stop.

What it turned on

Not a campaign. A brand that could survive a yard sign.

Local service work does not run on impressions. It runs on the sign at the end of the driveway, the hanger on the door, the flyer in the hand. The identity had to hold at that size, in that weather, from a car.

The A2 turn above is written from what the work plainly is: a brand built for print and signage rather than for campaigns. If that is not how you remember deciding it, say so and it comes out. It is the one beat on this page I have inferred.

What this costs

Six turns and five closes, one or two sentences each, from you. That is the whole bill. Everything else on these pages is already written.

And it is worth saying what the turn is not: it is not a summary of the approach. It is the thing that could have gone the other way. If I can work it out from the rest of the page, it is not a turn — which is exactly what went wrong the last time I tried to write them.
