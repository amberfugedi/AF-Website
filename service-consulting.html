Consulting

The hardest part usually isn’t the plan. It’s knowing what’s actually in the way.

Most companies I talk to already have ideas about what to do next. What they don’t have is agreement about which problem they’re solving.

I work with businesses on one defined marketing problem or initiative: finding what’s really causing it, deciding what to do about it, and staying close enough to the work that the answer holds up when the team builds it.

When it’s worth bringing someone in

Not because the team isn’t good. Usually because everyone is too close to it to see the shape of the problem.

  • Marketing is running and nobody can say clearly what it’s producing.
  • The team knows something needs to change but can’t agree on what.
  • A campaign, website, or launch needs a stronger strategy behind it than it currently has.
  • Tactics keep getting added while the underlying problem stays where it was.
  • You have real marketing capability in-house and need an outside read on it.
  • There’s one specific thing to fix, and it doesn’t warrant an ongoing leader.

A lot of marketing problems look tactical until you get close enough to see what’s causing them.

How I think

Solve the right problem first.

The requested deliverable and the actual problem are not always the same thing, and the gap between them is usually where the money goes.

  • The brief is a symptom

    Someone asks for a campaign because leads are down. The campaign is the request. It is not necessarily the cause.

  • Look past the channel

    Leads fall because the offer stopped being competitive, the follow-up takes four days, or the site never explains what the company actually does.

  • Say it early

    I would rather name the real problem up front than build the campaign and watch it underperform for reasons that were there before we started.

If the answer is fixing the offer instead of launching another campaign,that’s where we start.

How it works

From problem to a built solution.

The recommendation isn’t the finish line. I help work out what’s actually standing in the way, what will make the biggest difference, and how to bring it to life.

  1. Understand the problem

    Goals, context, and what’s really happening.

  2. Find what moves the needle

    Data, experience, and the real opportunities.

  3. Build the plan

    Prioritize the work and make it actionable.

  4. Bring it to life

    Support execution and remove roadblocks.

Real insights. Tangible progress.

What should be different

Every engagement is scoped differently. This part isn’t.

Before
  • Too many priorities.
  • Marketing activity without a clear strategy.
  • A vague problem everyone describes differently.
  • Recommendations that stay in a document.
After
  • A decision about what actually matters.
  • Work that connects to a specific business goal.
  • A defined issue the team knows how to solve.
  • A plan the team can actually build.

This is probably a fit if…

  • You have a specific marketing problem or initiative that needs senior attention.
  • Your team can execute but needs clearer direction on what to execute.
  • You’re making a significant marketing decision and want an outside read on it.
  • Marketing is busy and you’re not sure the right work is getting done.
  • You want someone strategic who’s also comfortable close to the execution.
  • You don’t need an ongoing marketing leader inside the business.
It probably isn’t a fit if…

You need the whole marketing function led rather than one problem solved. That’s fractional leadership, and it’s a different arrangement.

The engagement

Consulting is scoped as a project, shaped around the problem rather than a set number of hours. That might be an audit and a recommendation, a strategy engagement, a defined build, or short-term advisory around one initiative.

Most of it involves some hands-on work. I’ve found that the recommendations that survive are the ones I helped put into practice.
