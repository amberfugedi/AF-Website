The Work page is long. Three ways to deal with it.

Measured first. The category tabs already do most of the job: any single category is about three screens on a laptop. It is the default “All work” view that runs long, and it runs longest on a phone.

Of the 6,826px, 702px is section padding and the head, tab bar and closing CTA account for another 1,134px before a single project appears. The content itself is 26 entries.

A — Leave it, land somewhere shorter

No structural change. The tab bar stays, but the page opens on Marketing leadership & growth instead of All work, so the first thing a visitor meets is the two featured case studies and PERQ rather than 26 entries. “All work” is one click away and still the thing search engines and anyone without JavaScript see, because the other sections are in the page either way.

Cost: one line of JavaScript. Lands at 3.3 screens instead of 7.6. Risk: someone who wants the client work has to notice the tabs.

Nothing to preview here. It is the live page with a different starting tab: open the Work page

B — Collapsed categories (your dropdown idea)

Each category becomes a row you open. The page becomes four headings with counts, so the whole portfolio fits on one screen and you choose what to expand. Native <details>, so it works without JavaScript and everything stays in the HTML for search.

What it costs: nothing is visible until someone clicks. A visitor who lands here sees four labels and no work. It also reintroduces the open-and-close interaction you had taken off this page.

All four closed: about 1.6 screens on a laptop, counting the page head and the closing CTA. Opening one category adds roughly what that category costs today.

C — One compact index, everything visible

The opposite move. Instead of hiding the 26 entries behind four doors, give every one of them a single line: category, name, one phrase, and a link where there is a page. The whole portfolio is scannable in about two screens without a click, and the tabs still filter it.

What it costs: the featured case studies lose their raised treatment unless they keep a block above this list, and the three figures on the hub have nowhere to sit. This reads as a contents page, which suits a recruiter and suits a prospective client less.

And either way

702px of the current page is section padding, and three of the four joints between categories are 208px. The design system already allows a tighter pairing where sections are one argument, which four filtered categories are. That is about 240px off the desktop page and 150px off the phone, with no content change and no new interaction. It is worth doing whichever option you pick.
