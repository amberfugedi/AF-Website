Gutter cleaning

Clogged gutters overflow, and the water ends up against your siding, your foundation, and your front steps. We clear out leaves and debris so rain goes where it should.

What does gutter cleaning include?

Gutter cleaning removes leaves, pine needles, seed pods, and built-up debris from the gutter troughs, then checks that each downspout drains. How A2 cleans gutters: by hand, with a gutter vacuum, or both, and whether debris is bagged and hauled away

Why gutters matter

Gutters carry roof water away from the house. When they fill with leaves, water spills over the edge instead. That overflow can:

  • Soak the ground along your foundation and find its way into basements
  • Stain siding and wash out mulch beds below the roof line
  • Freeze in winter and add weight that pulls gutters away from the fascia
  • Hold damp debris against the roof edge and fascia boards

What we clean

  • Gutter troughs on every side of the house
  • Downspouts, checked so water flows through
  • Debris removal and cleanup from the yard and driveway

Dark streaks on the outside face of gutters are a separate job. Those come off with a house wash, and we can do both on the same visit.

Whether A2 repairs gutters, reattaches loose sections, or installs gutter guards

When to book

In Connecticut, most homes need gutters cleaned in late fall, after the leaves come down, and again in spring to clear seed pods and needles. Homes under oaks, maples, or pines often need a third cleaning.

Signs your gutters need attention: water spilling over the edge during rain, plants growing in the gutters, gutters sagging or pulling away, or a downspout that stays dry in a storm.

Gutter Cleaning questions

How often should gutters be cleaned?

Most Connecticut homes need it twice a year, in late fall and spring. Homes with heavy tree cover may need it more often.

Can you clean the outside of my gutters too?

Yes. The black streaks on the outside of gutters come off during a house wash, which can be booked with your gutter cleaning.

Do I need to be home?

Not usually, as long as we can reach every side of the house.
