Pressure washing vs. soft washing: which does your home need?

By A2 Exteriors · {{PUBLISH_DATE}}

Short answer

Pressure washing uses high water pressure to blast dirt off hard surfaces like concrete. Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution to kill and rinse away algae, mildew, and grime on surfaces high pressure can damage, like siding and roofs. Most homes need both, on different parts of the property.

How pressure washing works

A pressure washer pumps water through a narrow nozzle at high pressure. The force of the water does the cleaning. On concrete, that force lifts dirt, mildew, and tire marks out of the surface. Professionals often attach a rotary surface cleaner, a spinning bar under a round hood, so a whole driveway gets the same pressure and comes out even.

How soft washing works

Soft washing uses a pressure close to a garden hose. A cleaning solution does the work: it kills algae, mildew, and moss and loosens dirt, then a low-pressure rinse carries it away. Because the solution kills growth at the root, a soft-washed surface tends to stay clean longer than one that was only blasted.

Which surfaces get which method

SurfaceUsual method
Vinyl, aluminum, or fiber cement sidingSoft wash
Painted wood clapboard and trimSoft wash
StuccoSoft wash
Asphalt shingle roofSoft wash only
Concrete driveways and sidewalksPressure wash, usually with a surface cleaner
Pavers, brick, and natural stoneModerate pressure, adjusted to the material

What goes wrong with the wrong method

High pressure on vinyl siding can crack panels and drive water behind them, where it soaks insulation and sheathing. On painted wood it strips paint and raises the grain. On an asphalt roof it knocks off the granules that protect the shingles from the sun, which shortens the roof's life and can void the manufacturer's warranty.

Going too soft on concrete has the opposite problem. A low-pressure rinse leaves embedded dirt and tire marks in place.

Renting a pressure washer

A rental pressure washer works for small concrete jobs if you keep the nozzle moving and use a wide tip. Keep it off your siding, your roof, and anything painted. Ladder work with a pressure washer is a common way to get hurt, since the recoil can push you off balance.

When to call a professional

Call a professional for roofs, second stories, painted or older siding, and any time you are not sure what the surface can handle. Request a free quote and send a few photos.
