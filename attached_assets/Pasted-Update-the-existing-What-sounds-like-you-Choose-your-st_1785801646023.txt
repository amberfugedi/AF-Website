Update the existing “What sounds like you?” / “Choose your starting point” section on the Services page.

The current version has become too editorial and formal. It relies too heavily on oversized serif type, thin rules, large whitespace, and a manifesto-style content card.

Redesign it so it feels:

* warm
* practical
* approachable
* creative
* interactive
* easy to understand quickly

Aim for:

Designed, but not editorial. Creative, but not crafty. Clear enough to use immediately.

Keep

Keep the four options:

* No marketing yet
* It all depends on me
* The team is not aligned
* I need someone in my corner

Keep the interaction where only one option is active at a time and the supporting content updates based on the selection.

Change the visual hierarchy

Use the serif font only for the main section heading.

Use the site’s sans-serif font for:

* the four options
* supporting copy
* selected-state headline
* descriptions
* recommendations
* CTA

Do not use oversized serif type inside the selected content.

The selected-state headline should be clear and conversational, not styled like a second hero.

Replace the large content card

Remove the oversized boxed panel beneath the selector.

Instead, connect the active option directly to a compact response area.

The response area should include:

* one short headline
* one concise description
* a small “How I help” section
* best place to start
* CTA
* one subtle visual or annotation tied to the selected state

Keep the response visually open and integrated with the page rather than placing it inside a large card.

Add warmth and personality

Use restrained design details such as:

* small paper-note accents
* handwritten-style annotations used sparingly
* a soft butter highlight
* coral arrows or underlines
* subtle pink or lavender background shapes
* compact working-document details
* gentle movement when the active state changes

Do not use four full illustrations or separate hero images.

The visual detail should support the selected state without becoming the main content.

Selector design

Keep the four options easy to scan and tap.

Do not present them as:

* giant editorial headlines
* conventional cards
* filled pills
* an accordion
* a timeline
* a fashion-magazine contents page

Use simple horizontal or stacked choices with enough personality to feel custom.

The active option should use:

* stronger sans-serif typography
* a coral accent
* one small butter highlight or annotation
* a subtle shift in background or spacing

Inactive options should remain clearly readable.

Mobile design

The current mobile section is too tall.

On mobile:

* keep the section heading concise
* stack the four options
* keep the selected response compact
* avoid a giant card
* avoid large serif copy
* show the CTA without requiring excessive scrolling
* keep the entire interaction useful within roughly one to one-and-a-half phone screens when possible

Tone

The experience should feel like Amber is helping the visitor recognize their situation and choose a practical next step.

It should not feel like:

* a luxury editorial spread
* a software product selector
* a personality quiz
* a formal consulting framework
* a scrapbook collage

Preserve the current site colors and overall brand, but make the section friendlier, lighter, and more usable.
