House washing for Connecticut homes

Green film on the north wall, gray streaks under the gutters, dirt splashed up from the garden beds. We wash siding and trim with low pressure and a cleaning solution, the method siding is built to handle.

What is house washing?

House washing cleans the outside walls of your home: siding, trim, soffits, and the outer faces of gutters. Most siding gets cleaned with low pressure and a solution that kills algae and mildew, followed by a rinse. High pressure is the wrong tool for most siding because it can push water behind panels and strip paint.

What a house wash covers

  • Siding on every side of the house
  • Trim, soffits, and fascia
  • Outer faces of gutters and downspouts
  • Shutters, light fixtures, and the front entry area

Window glass gets rinsed along with the siding. A house wash is not a window cleaning service, so expect clean glass, not streak-free glass.

Problems it fixes

Connecticut summers stay humid, and shaded walls stay damp long after the sun dries the rest of the house. That is where you see it first:

  • Green or black algae, usually on north-facing walls and under trees
  • Mildew in gray or black spots, often near decks and downspouts
  • Pollen, dirt, and cobwebs collected under eaves and around lights
  • Mud splash along the bottom row of siding above mulch beds

Some marks need more than washing. Rust streaks, paint overspray, and the chalky film on aging vinyl or aluminum may lighten but not disappear. We point those out before we start.

How we clean it

We match the method to the surface. On most homes that means a soft wash:

  1. Wet down plants and shrubs around the house. Plant protection steps
  2. Apply a cleaning solution at low pressure, starting from the bottom and working up.
  3. Give it a few minutes to break down algae and mildew.
  4. Rinse from the top down with clean water, then rinse the plants again.

What to expect on the day

  • Before we arrive: close windows, bring in pets, and move toys, cushions, and anything fragile away from the walls.
  • Water: we connect to an outdoor faucet, so please make sure one works.
  • Time: most single-family homes take two to four hours.
  • After: siding dries within an hour or two in sun. You can open windows once we finish.

When to book

Late spring, after the pollen settles, is the most common time. A wash also makes sense before you list a house, before a big event at home, or any time you see green spreading on a wall. Most homes need it every one to two years.

House Washing questions

Can pressure washing damage siding?

Yes. High pressure can crack vinyl, force water behind siding and into wall cavities, strip paint, and splinter wood. That is why siding gets a low-pressure wash with a cleaning solution, and high pressure stays on concrete.

How often should a house be washed?

Most homes need a wash every one to two years. Houses under trees, near wetlands, or with walls that stay in shade often need it every year.

Do I need to be home during the wash?

Not usually. If we can reach a working outdoor faucet and get through any gates, you can be at work.
