How often should you wash your house in Connecticut?

By A2 Exteriors · {{PUBLISH_DATE}}

Short answer

Most Connecticut homes do well with a house wash every one to two years. Homes under trees, near wetlands, or with walls that stay shaded and damp often need a wash every year. Roofs and concrete run on their own schedules.

Signs your house is due

  • Green or black film on the north side of the house or under trees
  • Gray or black spots of mildew around decks, downspouts, and corners
  • A yellow-green dust of pollen that stays after a rain
  • Mud splashed up on the lowest rows of siding
  • Cobwebs and dirt collected under the eaves and around outdoor lights

How Connecticut weather adds up

Spring brings a heavy coat of tree pollen, usually peaking in May. Summer humidity keeps shaded walls damp, which is when algae and mildew spread fastest. Fall drops leaves that stain concrete and clog gutters. Winter adds road salt and sand to driveways and walks. A house that gets a lot of sun and sits away from trees may go two years between washes. A house in the woods may need one every spring.

The best time of year to book

Late spring through early fall works best. Cleaning solutions work better in mild temperatures, and surfaces need to dry. Most companies stop washing once overnight temperatures drop near freezing. Booking in late spring, after the pollen settles, gets the house clean for summer.

Other parts of the house

  • Roof: clean when streaks or moss appear. Many roofs go several years between cleanings.
  • Driveway and walks: once a year or every other year, often in spring or after fall leaves.
  • Patios: once a year before outdoor season, more often in heavy shade.
