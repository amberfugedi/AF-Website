Fractional leadership

Stronger marketing doesn’t always start with more marketing.

Sometimes the team is already there. So are the tools, agencies, campaigns, and budget.

But priorities keep shifting. The team stays busy. Leadership isn’t sure what’s actually working. Planning is reactive. Ownership gets blurry. And adding another tactic probably isn’t going to fix it.

I step in as a senior marketing leader to help strengthen the function itself: the direction, people, systems, decisions, and operating rhythm behind the work.

Talk through what’s happening See how I work

You may not need another marketing hire.

You may need a clearer way for the marketing you already have to work.

  • A capable team that spends most of its time reacting.
  • Too many priorities and no clear way to decide what comes first.
  • Reporting that shows activity without showing what it produced.
  • Work spread across employees, agencies, freelancers, and tools without clear ownership.
  • Budget being spent because it always has been rather than because it is still working.
  • Sales and marketing working toward slightly different definitions of success.
  • A marketing leader who needs stronger systems, structure, or support.
  • Good people inside a system that makes their jobs harder than it needs to be.

These aren’t always campaign problems. They’re often marketing-function problems.

What fractional leadership looks like

Not a strategy deck handed over from the outside. I work close enough to the business and the team to see how the work actually happens and where it gets stuck.

  • Direction and priorities

    Getting clear on where the team is going and what deserves attention now.

    • Marketing strategy
    • Planning
    • Prioritization
    • Decision-making

  • People and accountability

    Clearer ownership, and a team that works better together than it did.

    • Roles
    • Ownership
    • Team development
    • Operating rhythm

  • Resources and execution

    Making better use of the people, budget, partners and tools already in place.

    • Budget
    • Agencies and partners
    • Tools
    • Hiring and resourcing
    • Moving the work forward

  • Systems and performance

    The infrastructure to run marketing, measure it, and improve on it.

    • CRM and reporting
    • Marketing operations
    • Sales and marketing alignment
    • Workflow
    • Practical AI

The goal is not to make the marketing function bigger. It’s to make it work better.

Some of it is just being in the room.

Often the most useful thing is a senior marketing leader who can make sense of the tradeoffs, challenge the assumptions, and help the business make a better decision.

  • Helping leadership see what marketing is actually producing.
  • Pushing back on an agency or an outside partner.
  • Working out whether the next move is a hire, a tool, a strategy change, or nothing at all.
  • Backing a marketing leader who is already there, and deciding what shouldn’t be a priority.

How it works

This is an ongoing role rather than a project, so it is less a sequence than the order things tend to happen in.

  • Get inside the business

    Understand the goals, team, tools, budget, and how decisions actually get made.

  • Find the real constraints

    Identify what is creating drag, and what needs attention first.

  • Lead alongside the team

    Put structure around the work and stay close enough to help it move.

  • Make it usable without me

    Document, teach, and build with the team so the function gets stronger.

What should be different

The exact work varies by company. The direction doesn’t.

Before
  • Marketing is constantly reacting.
  • Ownership and priorities are unclear.
  • Reporting shows activity.
  • More budget feels like the default answer.
After
  • The team knows what matters most.
  • Responsibilities and decisions are clearer.
  • Leadership can see what is actually producing value.
  • Existing people, spend, and tools work harder.

This is probably a fit if…

  • There’s marketing activity, but no senior marketer owning the whole function.
  • You have marketers, junior or otherwise, and agencies, and nobody joining it up.
  • Marketing feels reactive, and leadership is spending too much time inside day-to-day decisions.
  • Priorities keep changing and there’s activity without much visibility into what’s working.
  • You’re not sure whether the answer is a hire, an agency, a system, or a change in strategy.
  • You need senior marketing leadership but aren’t ready for, or don’t need, another full-time executive.
  • You want someone senior enough to lead and close enough to the work to know what’s actually happening.

Probably not the right fit if…

You want someone to execute a predetermined list of tasks without questioning the strategy behind them. The value here is in looking across the function and changing what actually needs it.

The engagement

Engagements are usually structured as an ongoing monthly leadership role, with the level of involvement shaped around the team, the business, and what needs to change.

Some companies need that indefinitely. Others need someone to step in for a period, strengthen the function, coach the team, and help decide what leadership should look like next.

I keep the number of retained engagements small so I can stay close to the work.
