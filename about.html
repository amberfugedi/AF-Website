I’ve never been very good at staying inside the marketing box.

I’m Amber. I’ve spent more than a decade in marketing, but the work I’m most drawn to usually starts somewhere between marketing, sales, operations, technology, and the question nobody has asked yet.

Marketing leader. Strategist. Builder. Teacher.

How I got here

I didn’t have a perfectly mapped-out plan to end up in marketing.

I got into marketing by doing the work, saying yes to things I hadn’t done before, and figuring them out as I went.

What pulled me in was the mix of creativity and problem-solving. Marketing could mean writing something, building something, understanding people, looking at data, or trying to figure out why something wasn’t working.

The more I learned, the less interested I became in staying inside one narrow version of marketing. I wanted to understand sales. Operations. Systems. Customer behavior. Technology. What happened after the lead came in. Why one process worked and another didn’t.

I kept taking on bigger problems, getting closer to the decisions behind the work, and learning more of the business around marketing. That evolution is what shaped the way I think now.

That curiosity became the through-line in my career.

Why I started doing this on my own

I wanted marketing advice to feel more honest.

The longer I worked in marketing, the more I noticed how easy it was for businesses to be told they needed more marketing without anyone stopping to ask whether it was the right marketing.

Post more.

Send more emails.

Run more ads.

Buy the social media package.

Add another tool.

But more activity does not automatically mean more progress.

I kept thinking about the business on the other side of that advice. If marketing is not your world, how are you supposed to know whether an expensive social media package is actually the best use of your money?

What if that same budget would do more for you as:

  • A better website, or clearer messaging.
  • A stronger referral process, or better follow-up.
  • Fixing what happens after someone becomes a lead.

That disconnect is a big part of why I started consulting and eventually creating courses. Not “here are all the things you should be doing,” but “what does this business actually need right now?”

Sometimes that means doing more. Sometimes it means doing something completely different. And sometimes it means telling someone they don’t need the thing they thought they came to me for.

I wanted to make good marketing advice more accessible, more practical, and more honest.

How I think

I usually start with the problem underneath the problem.

I ask a lot of questions because I want to understand why something is happening before I decide what should change.

Give me a website problem and I’ll probably start asking about sales. Tell me you need more leads and I’ll want to know what happens after they come in.

I like strategy. I just don’t want to get so far away from the work that I stop knowing whether it actually works.

I want people to understand why I’m recommending something. I’ll tell you what I think you need, and I’ll also tell you when I don’t think you need something.

I like figuring out what is actually going on, then making it better.

What I believe

What I keep coming back to.

Facts over feelings.

I like opinions. I just like them more when we can test them.

Strategy only matters if it gets built.

I’ve never been very interested in recommendations that only work on a slide.

Make the work lighter.

If something can be simpler, I’m probably going to ask why it isn’t.

How I lead

The people part matters to me, too.

I got meaningful experience early in my career because someone trusted me with real work before I had years of experience proving I could do it.

I’ve never forgotten what that did for me.

That’s why I’ve always made time for interns, mentoring, and people earlier in their careers. I want them in the conversation. I want them to understand why decisions are being made, try things themselves, get feedback, and build confidence.

I think investing in people is one of the best uses of my time.

I want people to leave more capable than when we started.

What keeps me interested

What keeps me interested isn’t really marketing.

It’s figuring things out.

The moment when something complicated finally makes sense.

When the numbers explain what everyone has been feeling.

When the team knows what to do next.

When something that wasn’t working starts working.

I like the part where curiosity turns into clarity and someone can actually do something with it.

I also like the creative side of the work. The blank-page moment when an idea can become a campaign, a website, a better process, a useful AI tool, or something nobody had quite thought to build yet.

I like improving workflows, experimenting with technology, and building things that make work easier or more useful.

That combination of creativity, curiosity, technology, and real-world problem solving is what keeps the work interesting to me.

Outside the work

And outside the marketing of it all…

I’ve always had a lot of ideas.

That probably explains the side hustles, the half-built concepts, the websites, the notes full of things I may or may not ever come back to, and the fact that I’m usually thinking about what something could become next.

I like making things. Writing, building, designing, figuring out how a vague idea turns into something real that another person can actually use.

I also like learning for the sake of learning. I can go pretty far down a rabbit hole once I decide I want to understand something, which is probably why I ask so many questions. Most of the time, I’m trying to get to the why.

And I turn a lot of those questions on myself, too.

Growth matters to me. I care about learning from experience, changing my mind when I need to, and investing in myself just as intentionally as I invest in the work and the people around me.

I care deeply, too. When I believe in something or someone, I get invested. That includes the people I mentor, the work I choose to do, the ideas I decide are worth building, and the people in my life.

I also believe in taking care of myself. Fitness, movement, yoga, and my mental health are important parts of my life.

I’m ambitious, but I don’t think burnout is a personality trait.

I know by now that I’m better at everything else when I’m actually taking care of myself.

And I like having a life outside of work. I love traveling, good food, and the kind of sunset that makes you stop what you’re doing for a minute. I’m based in Connecticut, have two dogs, and have fostered dogs over the years too.

I think a lot of that comes down to what I choose to invest in: the things I’m learning, the ideas I’m building, the people I care about, and the kind of person I’m trying to become.

I want to leave things better than I found them.

A business. A team. Someone’s career. A process. Myself.

I’m not always going to get that right. But it matters to me that I keep trying.
