# Content gap analysis: the flagship case study

`/projects/marketing-function-next-stage`, reviewed 2026-11-20 against
the question the audit raised: does this page describe a leadership
philosophy, or an account of leadership?

No design change is proposed. Nothing here invents a fact.

---

## The finding that governs everything below

**The evidence that would make this page credible already exists on
this site, and it is on `/projects/perq`, one click away in this page's
own Related work list.**

PERQ carries: the dates, two promotions, the title sequence, ownership
of the function from the end of 2024, a seat on the Executive
Leadership Team, and five approved figures. This page carries none of
it, by design.

That is the whole gap in one line. It is not a writing problem. **An
anonymized account of leading a marketing function cannot borrow the
proof from the named account of the same engagement without
de-anonymizing itself by correlation** — same remit, same five
workstreams, same closing sentence about measuring a year, linked to
each other from both pages.

So there are two routes, and the choice is yours before any of the
section work below is worth doing:

**Route A — keep it anonymous.** The page can gain credibility only
from structural facts that carry no identifiers: scale in ranges, how
many roles, how long, what the team could do afterwards. Real
improvement, bounded ceiling. It will never be as persuasive as PERQ.

**Route B — name it.** PERQ is already named on this site with figures.
If this page named it too, every fact on PERQ becomes available to it,
and the two pages become one strong case study instead of one strong
and one abstract. The cost is that you lose the piece that currently
reads as transferable method rather than one employer's story — which
is exactly what a consulting prospect is buying.

My read: **Route B for credibility with recruiters, Route A for
consulting positioning.** The honest third option is to keep this page
anonymous and stop asking it to carry proof, letting PERQ do that job,
and tighten this one into the method piece it already nearly is. Say
which and I will work to it.

Everything below assumes Route A, since it is the current decision.

---

## Priority 1 — Scope and authority are entirely absent

**What the copy claims.** "Owner of the marketing function." That is the
whole statement of scope on the page.

**Why it is the first gap.** An executive recruiter reads a case study
to answer one question before any other: *how big was this, and what
was actually hers?* The page never says how many people, how much
budget, how long, or who she answered to. Without that, "owner of the
marketing function" could describe a team of two or a team of twenty,
and a reader assumes the smaller one.

**What evidence would fix it.** Any three of: team size at the start and
at the end; number of roles designed; the marketing budget as a range or
an order of magnitude; the reporting line; the period in months or
years.

**Already supported.** All of it, on PERQ and in your own history. The
seat on the Executive Leadership Team, the promotion sequence and the
December 2023 start date are published and approved.

**Needs your input.** Which of these you are willing to state in a form
that does not name the company. Headcount and budget scale are not
published anywhere on the site today, in any form.

**Can it be said without disclosure?** Yes, for most of it. "A team that
went from X to Y", "a budget in the low seven figures", "about two
years", "reported to the CEO", "sat on the executive team" are all
non-identifying on their own. The combination of an exact budget figure
and an exact headcount starts to narrow the field; ranges do not.

## Priority 2 — The five workstreams state principles, not decisions

**What the copy claims.** Each workstream opens with a maxim in the
imperative:

> Start from the business plan, not the marketing plan.
> Design the roles before filling them.
> Write the operating model down, with the people who use it.
> Make performance readable before asking anyone to act on it.
> Put the investment case in the same language as the rest of the business.

Counting first-person action across all five: **two of five.** Only
Organizational design ("I drew the roles the function would need…") and
Marketing operations ("I designed it and put it in place together with
the person who would run it every day") say what you did. The other
three describe the practice impersonally.

**Why it matters.** A maxim is advice for someone else. It demonstrates
that you know how the work should go, which is what My Approach is for.
It does not demonstrate that you did it.

**What evidence would fix it, per workstream.**

| | Needs |
|---|---|
| **Strategy and business alignment** | What the business plan actually asked of marketing, in shape not numbers. What marketing stopped doing as a result — the thing the shorter plan left out. |
| **Organizational design** | How many roles you drew. How many already existed, how many people grew into one, how many you hired. What at least one of those roles was. |
| **Marketing operations** | What was in the model. What broke before it existed. Whether it survived the handover the copy says it was built for — that is a verifiable outcome and the page implies it without claiming it. |
| **Marketing intelligence** | What "a week to assemble" became. What a leader can now open that they could not before. |
| **Investment and planning** | What the planning cycle looked like before and after. Whether a recommendation you built was adopted, and what it changed about the allocation. |

**Already supported.** Marketing operations and Investment are both
documented in detail on `/projects/marketing-operations` — the model was
built with the team's marketing operations specialist, it covers
objectives, the work beneath them and ownership in one place, and the
planning work is benchmarking, allocation modelling and scenario-running
against the revenue target, taken as a recommendation to the leaders who
weigh it. Marketing intelligence is documented on
`/projects/business-intelligence`. **Three of the five workstreams can be
made concrete from pages already live on this site, without asking you
for anything.**

**Needs your input.** Strategy and Organizational design. Specifically:
the count of roles drawn, grown and hired; and what the business plan
asked marketing for.

**Can it be said without disclosure?** Yes. Role counts and the shape of
an operating model disclose nothing proprietary. The line to hold is the
one the brief draws: no internal workflows, templates, prompts, campaign
configurations or the model's actual contents — *that* the model covers
ownership and prioritization is already public on another page; *how* it
does is not.

## Priority 3 — "What changed" asserts outcomes it does not evidence

**What the copy claims.** Five before-and-after rows. Roles redrawn,
knowledge out of individual heads, performance in one place, the plan
written from the business plan, investment in commercial language.

**Why it matters.** These are the page's results section, and every row
is a description of a new state rather than evidence that the state was
reached. "Performance in one place, with the age of every figure
visible" is a claim about a system. Nothing confirms it exists, is used,
or replaced anything.

**What evidence would fix it.** One verifiable detail per row. For
performance: who opens it and how often. For the operating model:
whether a handover actually happened. For planning: whether the next
year's budget was built the new way.

**Already supported.** The reporting application is documented on
`/projects/business-intelligence` as something you designed and built,
on top of existing reporting. That is enough to turn one row from a
claim into a fact.

**Needs your input.** The other four, at one sentence each.

**Can it be said without disclosure?** Yes. "The operating model outlived
my ownership of it" is a strong, non-identifying, verifiable claim —
if it is true. Do not let me write it otherwise.

## Priority 4 — The closing statement makes a claim the page never supports

**What the copy claims.**

> I measure it now by what the function can do that it could not do
> before, and by how many of the people in it are ready for a job bigger
> than the one they have.

**Why it matters.** It is the best sentence on the page and the one most
likely to be quoted back at you in an interview. It names two measures
and then reports neither. A reader who takes it seriously immediately
asks: so how many?

**What evidence would fix it.** A number of people promoted, or who
moved into a larger role, or who left for a bigger job elsewhere with
your support. No names needed.

**Already supported.** Nothing on the site records team outcomes.

**Needs your input.** The count, and whether you are willing to publish
it.

**Can it be said without disclosure?** Yes, as a count. "Three of the
people on that team are now doing jobs bigger than the one they had"
names nobody and is the single most persuasive sentence this page could
gain. It is also the hardest for a competitor to copy, which is a reason
to want it.

## Priority 5 — "Leading the function. Staying close to the work."

**What the copy claims.** That you sat in the rooms where the business
made its plans, and that the two columns are one job rather than two.

**Why it matters.** The audit was right that this is conceptually the
strongest section, and it is the one recruiters evaluating you for a
hands-on VP role will read hardest. It is currently two credible lists
with no instance attached to either.

**What evidence would fix it.** One occasion. A single time you set
direction and also built the first version of the thing, named at the
level of "I wrote the first version of X because it did not exist yet."

**Already supported.** The reporting application is exactly this, and it
is already published: you built it yourself, with Claude, a feature at a
time. It belongs in this section and is not currently referenced here.

**Needs your input.** Nothing, for that one. A second instance would
strengthen it.

**Can it be said without disclosure?** Yes. It is already live on another
page of this site.

---

## Priority 6 — Two display lines are pure philosophy

**What the copy claims.**

> A growing business outgrows the shape its marketing was built for, and
> it usually happens while the campaigns are still working.

> Growth puts weight on the parts of a function nobody looks at.

**Why it is last.** Both are good, and both belong to My Approach's job
rather than this one. They are chapter headings, so they are also the
cheapest thing on the page to leave alone. I would not touch them until
Priorities 1 to 5 are done, because once the page has evidence around
them they read as a leader's framing of her own work rather than as
general advice. **Philosophy next to evidence is credibility.
Philosophy on its own is a blog post.**

1. Attach the reporting application to the Leadership section, drawing
   on `/projects/business-intelligence`.
2. Make Marketing operations and Investment concrete from
   `/projects/marketing-operations`, where the detail is already

That is roughly a third of the gap, and it changes no design and invents
nothing.

## What only you can supply, in order of value

1. Scale: team size, budget order of magnitude, period, reporting line.
2. Team outcomes: how many people moved up.
3. Role counts: drawn, grown into, hired.
4. Whether the operating model survived a handover.
5. What the business plan asked of marketing, and what marketing
   stopped doing because of it.

Five answers. None of them requires a number you have not already lived,
and none of them names the company.
